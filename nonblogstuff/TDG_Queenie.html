Decision-Making Scenario

Against the Wall, Again

Corrugated-metal warehouse, Tai Tong Organic EcoWorld, near Yuen Long. Round two.

Objective Win the bout.
Opponent Queenie “Freestyle” Wai
Format Decided on points
Her stick Foam-wrapped, about 1.5 m long; approved by the referee
Arena About 10 m × 10 m of gym mats running to corrugated-metal walls, no ropes
Clock Second round, 1 minute remaining
Score Not closely tracked; few points scored so far
Position Queenie has her back to the wall; you hold the centre

1.Situation

You are a boxer fighting Queenie “Freestyle” Wai inside a corrugated-metal warehouse at Tai Tong Organic EcoWorld, near Yuen Long. The bout is decided on points. Your objective is to win the bout.

2.Terrain

The fighting surface is roughly ten metres square, covered in navy-blue gym mats extending to the warehouse walls. There are no ropes. Around thirty spectators watch from chairs beyond the mats. A referee supervises the fight; a bell and digital timer sit on the officiating table.

3.Your Opponent

Queenie is about your height. She wears a rumpled T-shirt, green knee-length shorts, leather knee and elbow pads, and scuffed white trainers. She carries a foam-wrapped stick approximately a metre and a half long. Before the fight, the referee tested it by striking her thigh and allowed her to keep it.

4.Her Movement

Queenie uses the stick to support her movement. So far, you have seen the following:

  • The swing: You have watched her plant the stick, transfer her weight through her arms and swing across your front. She can change direction faster than you can turn to follow. When she circles out of punching range, simply advancing behind your jab does little to close the distance.
  • The kick: She has planted the stick ahead and to your right, lifted both feet from the mat and swung around it into a kick. The stick flexes under her weight. Her movement can carry her out of range again before you reset.

5.Round Two

The second round is underway. Your shirt sticks to your back. Queenie is still moving energetically.

You have spent much of this round taking the centre and cutting off her lateral movement. Instead of following every change of direction, you have steadily reduced the space behind her.

A few moments ago, you appeared to have her cornered. Corrugated steel stood behind her, with less than a metre of mat between her feet and the wall. Without looking back, she reached behind herself and touched the metal.

You stepped in lightly, preparing to lead with your jab.

As you moved, Queenie planted the stick at her feet. Both hands pushed against it, sending her backwards and upwards. Her shoes landed against the wall above your head. She immediately drove off the metal, travelled out and across, and landed behind you.

Your gloves never reached her.

She was back in the centre.

6.Current Situation

You turned and worked her back again: step, step, turn. Now the corrugated wall is behind her once more. You face her from the open mat, with the centre behind you. Space remains along the wall to either side.

Her weight shifts forward. The stick’s tip begins to fall towards the mat.

She has not yet planted it. You have seen the wall escape once. You do not know whether she will repeat it, change direction or respond to what you do.

One minute remains in the round. You have not kept close track of the score, but few points have been scored so far.

Requirement

Describe your next three to five seconds. Specify your first foot movement, where you direct your attention, and when and where you intend to punch. What observable cue would make you change your action?
